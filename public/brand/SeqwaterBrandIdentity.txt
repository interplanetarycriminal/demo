Identity at a glance
Trusted government utility. Community-first, safety-first, quietly proud of infrastructure scale. Tagline "Water for life" does a lot of the positioning work, it shows up in the logo lockup itself. The voice is plain-English, service-oriented, and shows custodianship over cleverness. Acknowledgement of Country sits prominently in the footer, not buried. This is a brand built to reassure 3.5 million people that their tap water is handled by grown-ups.
Colour palette
Primary is a bright water-blue (roughly in the #00A9E0 to #00BCE4 range, sampled from the logo). Supporting darker navy for body copy and structural elements (around #003A5D / #002C4D territory). Whites and very light greys for backgrounds. Occasional teal or sky accent. Very restrained use of warm colours, red is reserved for emergency contact and safety notices, which is load-bearing brand behaviour worth preserving in mockups. Confirm exact hexes by sampling the live logo SVG if this matters for pixel-accuracy.
Typography
Clean humanist sans-serif throughout. Feels like Open Sans, Source Sans, or similar public-sector-friendly family. Large, confident H1s ("About us"), descriptive tagline-style H2s under them ("Water is essential for life"). Body copy is generous, high-readability, not tight editorial.
Layout patterns
Top utility bar with About Us / News / Careers / Contact / Emergency Contact. Primary nav is deep and functional: Water Supply, Things To Do, Projects and Maintenance, Community, About us, each with a fat dropdown. A persistent secondary quick-access strip for the three things people actually want (Dam levels, Water quality, Recreation safety notices). Content pages lead with a bold statement heading, short intro para, then bulleted "What we do" / "Our commitment" style lists. Card grids for sub-sections and news teasers. News cards carry a category tag (Projects, Recreation) above the headline. Footer carries Acknowledgement of Country, quick links, social icons, contact form.
Imagery
Documentary and utilitarian, not stylised. Real infrastructure (cofferdams, cable anchors, treatment plants), real people doing real water recreation, real safety campaigns. No stock gloss, no abstract AI-generated hero art. If you're mocking up, lean into wide landscapes of SEQ dams and engineering detail shots rather than metaphor imagery.
Voice and tone
Factual, warm, slightly formal. Uses "we" heavily ("We deliver safe, reliable..."). Leans on concrete numbers as credibility (3.5 million people, 26 dams, 600 km of pipelines, 73,000 hectares). Never boastful, always framed as duty. Plain verbs, short sentences, active voice.
Component cues for your mockup
Rounded but not bubbly buttons. Card corners on the softer side. Blue-on-white primary CTAs. Icon style is simple flat line/fill, see the social icons in the footer for reference. Status and dam-level components are a signature element worth including if the mockup wants to feel on-brand, even a simple horizontal bar with percentage capacity reads as Seqwater-flavoured.
