LOGICAL CLOCKS

Wall clocks skew, so ordering needs causality: max(local, received)+1 stamps every event. Lamport counters preserve cause→effect; vector clocks also reveal what is truly independent.

Tap a bead to light its causal past. In VECTOR mode tap two beads for a verdict.
Honesty: 3 processes, instantaneous local events, and no clock drift correction — real deployments run NTP/PTP and still cannot order two events closer together than their uncertainty.
Lineage. Lamport 1978, "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System" — the most cited paper in distributed computing — defined happened-before and the counter that respects it. Fidge and Mattern (1988) added vector clocks, which also detect concurrency. Their descendants are version vectors in Dynamo, dotted version vectors in Riak, and the causal metadata inside every CRDT.
