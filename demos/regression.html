Least Squares

Space play · ←/→ tour · R reset

Least squares picks the line whose error squares, one built on each point’s vertical miss, add up to the smallest total area.

    In the wild

    • Every regression head: price and demand forecasts, the value head of an RL agent, and reward models all train on squared error.
    • Diffusion models (image and video generators) learn by least squares: predict the added noise ε and minimise ‖ε − ε̂‖².
    • GPS and sensor fusion: your phone solves a least-squares problem to find where it is, and a Kalman filter is recursive least squares with a motion model. Object detectors switch to smooth-L1 for box regression so outliers can’t take over.

    Stable only if η < 2/λmax, where λmax is the bowl’s steepest curvature (the largest eigenvalue of 2⁄nXᵀX).

    5 · Absolute loss (L1)

    L₁ = 1⁄n Σ |ri| = …
    ∂L₁/∂wj = −1⁄n Σ sign(ri) xij
    L1 optimum ◆ = (…)

    sign(r) means every point pulls with the same force however far away it is, which is why one outlier can’t take over. There is no closed form, so the ◆ here is found by iteratively re-weighted least squares, polished to the exact corner of the kinked bowl (an L1 fit passes through d+1 of the points), and the amber dot uses sub-gradient steps that shrink over time.

    Least squares uses vertical gaps, not perpendicular distance. Swap the roles of x and y and you get a different line. The line nearest in perpendicular distance is total least squares, which is PCA.
    Squaring isn’t arbitrary. If the noise is Gaussian, minimising Σr² is exactly maximum likelihood, and it gives the bowl a single smooth bottom. It is also why a 3× miss costs 9× — the outlier problem.
