DBSCAN clustering

Space play · ←/→ tour · R reset

A point is core if at least minPts points lie within radius ε of it. Chain together cores that are within ε of each other and you get clusters of any shape, with no k to choose and the sparse leftovers labelled noise.

    In the wild

    • Maps & mobility: grouping GPS pings into stops, pickup hotspots and places (DBSCAN with haversine distance; ST-DBSCAN adds time).
    • Anomaly detection: the noise label is the product. Odd transactions, faulty sensors and intrusions sit in sparse regions.
    • Embeddings & 3-D: HDBSCAN, DBSCAN’s successor, clusters sentence embeddings in topic models like BERTopic; LiDAR pipelines use density clustering to split point clouds into obstacles.

    Everything DBSCAN does follows from one count: how many points sit inside a circle of radius ε.

    1 · The ε-neighbourhood

    Nε(p) = { q : ‖p − q‖ ≤ ε }
    ε = –

    It includes p itself (the scikit-learn convention), so minPts = 5 means p plus four others.

    2 · Three roles

    ● core    |Nε(p)| ≥ minPts (–) → –
    ○ border  not core, but p ∈ Nε(c)
              for some core c → –
    × noise   neither → –

    3 · Clusters are connected cores

    core c ~ core c′ ⇔ a chain of cores
    c = c1, …, cm = c′, ‖ci − ci+1‖ ≤ ε
    → – clusters

    Link every pair of cores closer than ε: each connected piece of that graph is one cluster, plus the borders it touches. The animation is a breadth-first search of the graph, and the violet rings are its queue.

    4 · Choosing ε: the k-distance

    dk(p) = distance to p’s k-th
            nearest neighbour
    k = minPts − 1 = –
    p is core ⇔ dk(p) ≤ ε (–) → –

    That count always equals the core count above: the amber line on the k-distance plot is the core / non-core split. Sorted, dk is flat inside clusters and shoots up for outliers, so the elbow is where “inside a cluster” ends.

    Borders depend on visiting order. Cores and noise are fixed by ε and minPts, but a border point within ε of two clusters joins whichever reaches it first.
    “No k” is not “no assumptions”. ε and minPts together say “a cluster is a region at least this dense”, one global density. Mixed densities break it (tour step 6); HDBSCAN removes ε by trying all of them.

    Cost: one range query per point, so O(n²) brute force as here, or roughly O(n log n) with a grid or k-d tree when ε is small.
