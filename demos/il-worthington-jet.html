Worthington Jet

The crater a drop digs collapses inward; all that converging water focuses on the axis and fires a thin jet upward — often higher than the drop fell — which then pinches off a satellite by Plateau–Rayleigh.

Model: axisymmetric toy — crater as a parabola with prescribed collapse, jet kinematics from geometric focusing. Not a Navier–Stokes solve.
A. M. Worthington photographed splashes by spark light from 1877 and published the plates as A Study of Splashes (1908). Lineage: Worthington's plates → Edgerton's strobes → cavity-collapse & bubble-jet physics → inkjet printheads and needle-free injectors that ride the same focusing.
