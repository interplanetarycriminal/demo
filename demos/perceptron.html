Perceptron Learning

Space play · ←/→ tour · R reset

A perceptron is one neuron: it takes a weighted vote of its inputs, and every time it is wrong it tilts its weights toward the right answer.

    In the wild

    • Inside every neural net. Each unit of a transformer's linear layers computes exactly this w·x + b vote; the hard threshold was swapped for smooth activations so gradients can flow.
    • Fast linear classifiers. Spam filters and click prediction on millions of sparse features, and the averaged / structured perceptron that powered NLP taggers and parsers through the 2000s.
    • Linear probes. Interpretability researchers fit a single linear classifier on a model's activations to ask: is this concept linearly separable in here?

    4 · Convergence theorem (Novikoff, 1962)

    If some line separates the data with margin γ and every point lies within radius R, then from w = 0 the perceptron makes at most (R/γ)² mistakes, in any order.

    η changes nothing here. From w = 0, w is always η × Σ y·x over past mistakes, and sign() ignores scale, so every decision (and the mistake count) is the same for any η > 0.
    Converged ≠ best. It stops at the first line with zero mistakes, often skimming one class. Maximising the margin is what an SVM adds; on non-separable data it never stops (pocket / averaged perceptrons fix that).
