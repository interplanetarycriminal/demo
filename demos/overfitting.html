Bias & Variance

Space play · ←/→ tour · R reset

A model can be wrong in two ways: too stiff to follow the truth (bias), or so bendy it follows the noise and changes with every new sample (variance). Test error is the two added together, plus noise nothing can remove.

    In the wild

    • Every validation curve. Train/validation splits and early stopping in deep learning read this same U — training steps or model size play the role of degree.
    • Weight decay. AdamW's decay term in LLM and vision training is the λ knob: a smooth dial on effective complexity.
    • Bagging & random forests average many high-variance models — literally the blue average-of-resamples curve — to cancel variance.

    1 · The model and how it is fitted

    ŷ(x) = Σk=0…d wk Pk(x)   d = –
    w = argmin Σi (yi − ŷ(xi))² + λ Σk≥1 wk²   λ = –

    Pk are Legendre polynomials: the same curves as 1, x, …, xd, but numerically stable. Solved exactly: (ΦᵀΦ + λI′)w = Φᵀy, where I′ is the identity with a 0 for the intercept w0.

    Training error can never tell you when to stop: with λ = 0 it is non-increasing in d, because a degree-d curve is just a degree-(d+1) curve with its last weight set to 0.

    3 · Where test error comes from

    E[(y − ŷ)²] = (f − Eŷ)² + E[(ŷ − Eŷ)²] + σ²
    – = bias² – + var – + σ² –

    E is over K = 160 training sets of n = – points redrawn from the same world, and over x across [−1, 1]. f is the hidden truth (dashed). The bar on the stage is this line.

    Variance is not the noise in the data. It measures how much the fitted curve itself would change if you had drawn a different sample. σ² is the floor no model can beat.
    Beyond the U — double descent. Set n below 16 in Controls: error spikes at d = n − 1, where the curve can pass exactly through every point, and can fall again past it. Giant neural nets live on that far side.
