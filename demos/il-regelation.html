Regelation · the wire through ice

A loaded wire raises the pressure beneath it, ice melts below at a lower melting point, the water flows around and refreezes above — the block heals behind the wire.

Michael Faraday named regelation in 1850; James Thomson predicted pressure-melting in 1849 and William Thomson measured it in 1857; J. T. Bottomley made the loaded-wire demo classic in 1872. The same physics is argued over today in glacier basal sliding.
