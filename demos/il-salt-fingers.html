Salt Fingers

Warm-salty over cold-fresh is stable in bulk — but heat diffuses ~100× faster than salt, so a parcel nudged down loses its heat, keeps its salt, turns heavy, and keeps sinking.

Model: coarse 2-D Boussinesq advection–diffusion of T & S on a 120×80 grid with a vorticity-free buoyancy-drag velocity closure — a cartoon of finger convection, not DNS.
Jevons (1857) noticed the curiosity; Melvin Stern (1960) named the "oceanographic perpetual salt fountain" and gave the instability its theory. Lineage: Jevons 1857 → Stern 1960 → thermohaline staircases in the Mediterranean outflow & Tyrrhenian Sea → C–O semiconvective staircases in stars. Push τ→1 and the fingers die: equal diffusivities, no engine.
